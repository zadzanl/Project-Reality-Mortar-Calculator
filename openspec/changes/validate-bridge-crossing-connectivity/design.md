## Context

The prior automatic bridge implementation mixed decoded mesh evidence, candidate masks, and vehicle routing. It was rolled back surgically because the bridge result was not reliable and the processor files also contain unrelated terrain and flow work. The replacement is an offline, auditable workflow over the 85 canonical `processed_maps/<map>/minimap.png` files.

The existing flow convention is authoritative: repository coordinates use a top-left origin, x increasing east, y increasing south; flow bytes are row-0-north; Leaflet displays the map over `[[0, 0], [map_size, map_size]]`. Annotation geometry must be stored in map/world units, with source image dimensions retained for audit.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- Produce a deterministic annotation file for every map, including reviewed no-bridge maps.
- Use a minimal rotated-rectangle geometry: center, angle, width, and length.
- Inspect every map at least twice in an annotator round and twice in an independent reviewer round.
- Keep human uncertainty and reviewer disagreement in comments/review records rather than forcing a confidence field into the core geometry schema.
- Derive the two sides of a crossing from rectangle orientation and validate their terrain connection before routing.
- Render annotations and validation diagnostics against the original minimap for visual audit.

**Non-Goals:**

- Inferring bridges from mesh names, static templates, flatness, or automatic collision extraction.
- Treating every pixel inside a rectangle as traversable without terrain and bank validation.
- Modifying raw archives, browser ballistics, boats, helicopters, infantry, or unrelated flow behavior.
- Claiming that a reviewed minimap rectangle proves in-game collision truth.

## Decisions

### 1. Rectangle-only core geometry

Store `map`, `map_size`, `image_size`, and `bridges`. Each bridge stores a stable local ID, `center_x`, `center_y`, `width`, `length`, and `angle_deg`. Four corners are derived at render/validation time, avoiding duplicate geometry that can drift. Pixel coordinates are derived from map size and image dimensions, not stored as a second source of truth.

Alternative rejected: storing only four pixels, because pixel-only records do not integrate directly with world-coordinate flow grids and vary with image resolution.

### 2. Comments and review records hold uncertainty

The core annotation record has no required confidence field. Annotators and reviewers write free-text comments and structured disposition records separately. A reviewer can approve, flag disagreement, correct geometry, or mark a suspected bridge as unresolved. The user adjudicates flagged disagreements before approval.

Alternative rejected: a required numeric confidence field, because it invites inconsistent scoring and does not replace visual evidence.

### 3. Deterministic overlapping assignments

Generate manifests from the sorted canonical map-directory list. Ten annotator identities and ten disjoint reviewer identities receive assignments generated by a validator. The validator proves every map has at least two assignments in each round and reports missing/under-covered maps. Reviewers receive the image and annotator output but record an independent first judgment before seeing the annotator disposition.

### 4. Geometry derives sides; terrain validates passability

The long rectangle axis defines the crossing direction. Two endpoint bands are derived just outside the rectangle along that axis. The pipeline samples passable terrain on both sides, rejects rectangles with no usable opposite-side approaches, and creates a connector only when the two sides are separated without the rectangle and connected through the approved rectangle. Rectangle interior is not an unconditional bank or water override.

Alternative rejected: blindly marking the entire rectangle traversable, because a minimap rectangle can include water, ramps, vegetation, or a non-traversable approach.

### 5. Separate human evidence from generated routing artifacts

Annotations and review logs are immutable inputs. Derived connector masks, QA images, and flow generations include hashes of their annotation and review inputs. Re-running with the same inputs must produce the same bytes.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **[Risk]** Minimap imagery may hide a bridge or make a road look like one. → Require two independent views, preserve comments, and escalate disagreements to the user.
- **[Risk]** A rectangle may be geometrically correct but not aligned with world coordinates. → Record image dimensions and map size, render a round-trip overlay, and reject out-of-bounds or transform-inconsistent records.
- **[Risk]** Automatic side derivation may choose the wrong bank on diagonal or wide rivers. → Keep the rectangle as the only annotation input but emit diagnostics and require user adjudication for flagged cases.
- **[Risk]** Batch overlap may silently omit a map. → Validate manifests against the canonical 85-map inventory before dispatch and before acceptance.
- **[Risk]** Existing dirty files can be damaged during integration. → Keep annotation work additive and use focused patches; do not restore or regenerate unrelated files.

## Migration Plan

1. Keep the archived automatic bridge change as historical evidence; do not sync its rejected delta specs.
2. Implement the annotation schema, manifest generator, and manifest validator without changing routing outputs.
3. Dispatch the ten annotators, collect all 85 map records, then dispatch ten independent reviewers.
4. Present disagreement records for user adjudication and freeze approved annotations.
5. Implement side derivation and connector validation, render QA overlays, and run synthetic tests.
6. Regenerate flow artifacts only for explicitly approved maps and compare protected non-bridge outputs.
7. Roll back by removing derived annotation/connector outputs; raw maps and unrelated artifacts remain untouched.

## Open Questions

- Whether a flagged annotation may be approved unchanged with an adjudication comment, or must be edited first.
- Which exact terrain-cell search radius best handles 4 m and 8 m simulation grids without inventing a bank.
